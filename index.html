THE ORGANIZATION

Build something that stays.

An independent organization is your preferred long-term direction: its own voice, donation accountability, and responsibility for the improvements it funds.

THE CREATIVE BOUNDARY

Be bold about the behavior.

  • Use irony, surprise, and a clear alternative.
  • Show a person choosing better, not a person being humiliated.
  • Never make poverty, a district, or a sanitation worker the punchline.
  • No filming or posting identifiable people to shame them.
  • Recognize care without inventing “everyone does it” statistics.

Test the tone with residents and sanitation workers before placing it in their street.

MEASURE WHAT CHANGES

Reach is a signal. A cleaner street is the result.

↘

Litter on the street

Count visible items on the same three 100 m segments, at the same times, three times weekly. Establish a baseline in days 1–14; compare weekly medians.

◷

Bins that work

Record present, usable, not-overflowing bins ÷ scheduled bin checks. Keep pickup completion and theft incidents in a separate log.

❞

Messages people retain

Use short, voluntary resident intercepts. Ask what they recall and what they would do without a bin. Do not treat stated intent as observed behavior.

Proposed decision rules: target ≥90% usable bin checks and ≥90% completed pickups; seek a 20% reduction in median litter counts by day 90. Set final targets after baseline. Record footfall, weather, cleanups, and events; use a matched comparison street if feasible. A small pilot cannot prove nationwide causality.

ADOPT A PLACE, NOT JUST A BIN

A small, cared-for street cluster.

  • Start near everyday activity: a shop cluster, school exit, or transport stop. Select the location after listening and a waste-flow audit.
  • Agree who empties each bin, where the waste goes, and what happens on a missed collection.
  • Use permission-approved, accessible placement; tamper-resistant fixings and replaceable parts with low resale value.
  • Fund modest improvements such as removing dumped waste or refreshing an approved planter. Keep walking routes open.
  • Give every asset an ID, named caretaker, condition log, and repair budget. Work with existing collectors.

THE INDEPENDENT ORGANIZATION

Let people see where the money goes.

Before collecting

Confirm the appropriate organizational and fundraising route with qualified local advice and relevant authorities. Get placement permissions and written service commitments.

Every contribution

Use approved payment channels, issue receipts, separate campaign funds, record restricted gifts, and require two-person spending approval.

Every month

Publish a simple ledger: money in, supplier invoices, asset IDs, operating costs, condition checks, and unspent balance. Share setbacks as well as improvements.

These are proposed governance controls. The organization, permissions, collection agreements, and fundraising arrangements are not yet established.
